SECTION 1: BASIC CONCEPTS OF PHYSICS (Q1–Q10)
Q1. What is Physics?
Answer: Physics is the branch of science that deals with the study of matter, energy, force, motion and their interactions.
In Nautical Science, Physics helps us understand how ships float, move, maintain stability and operate in water.
Q2. What are the main branches of Physics?
Answer: Some major branches of Physics are:
    1. Mechanics.
    2. Thermodynamics.
    3. Optics.
    4. Electromagnetism.
    5. Acoustics.
    6. Atomic Physics.
    7. Nuclear Physics.
    8. Fluid Mechanics.
Q3. What is a physical quantity?
Answer: A physical quantity is a property that can be measured and expressed using a numerical value and a unit.
Examples:
    • Length.
    • Mass.
    • Time.
    • Temperature.
    • Speed.
    • Force.
    • Pressure.
Q4. What is a fundamental quantity?
Answer: A fundamental quantity is an independent physical quantity that is not defined in terms of other physical quantities.
The seven SI fundamental quantities are:
Quantity	SI Unit
Length	Metre (m)
Mass	Kilogram (kg)
Time	Second (s)
Electric current	Ampere (A)
Temperature	Kelvin (K)
Amount of substance	Mole (mol)
Luminous intensity	Candela (cd)
Q5. What is a derived quantity?
Answer: A derived quantity is a physical quantity obtained by combining fundamental quantities mathematically.
Examples:
    • Speed = Distance / Time.
    • Acceleration = Velocity / Time.
    • Force = Mass × Acceleration.
    • Pressure = Force / Area.
Q6. What is the SI system?
Answer: SI stands for International System of Units.
It is the internationally accepted system of measurement used in science, engineering and many technical fields.
Q7. What is the difference between scalar and vector quantities?
Answer:
Scalar quantity: A physical quantity that has magnitude only.
Examples:
    • Mass.
    • Time.
    • Distance.
    • Speed.
    • Temperature.
    • Energy.
Vector quantity: A physical quantity that has both magnitude and direction.
Examples:
    • Force.
    • Velocity.
    • Displacement.
    • Acceleration.
    • Momentum.
Q8. What is magnitude?
Answer: Magnitude is the numerical size or value of a physical quantity, without considering its direction.
Example: A force of 50 Newtons has a magnitude of 50 N.
Q9. What is a unit?
Answer: A unit is a standard reference used to measure a physical quantity.
Examples:
    • Metre for length.
    • Kilogram for mass.
    • Second for time.
    • Newton for force.
    • Pascal for pressure.
Q10. Why is Physics important in Nautical Science?
Answer: Physics is important because it helps Deck Officers understand:
    1. How ships float.
    2. How ships move through water.
    3. How forces affect ships.
    4. How water pressure changes with depth.
    5. How gravity affects ship stability.
    6. How waves affect ships.
    7. How buoyancy works.
    8. How ships experience resistance in water.

SECTION 2: MECHANICS (Q11–Q20)
Q11. What is Mechanics?
Answer: Mechanics is the branch of Physics that deals with the behaviour of bodies under the action of forces and their motion.
It is particularly important in understanding the movement and stability of ships.
Q12. What are the main branches of Mechanics?
Answer: Mechanics is broadly divided into three branches:
    1. Statics: Study of bodies at rest or in equilibrium.
    2. Kinematics: Study of motion without considering the forces causing it.
    3. Dynamics: Study of motion and the forces responsible for it.
Q13. What is rest?
Answer: A body is said to be at rest when its position does not change with time relative to a specified reference point.
Example: A ship secured alongside a berth may be considered at rest relative to the berth.
Q14. What is motion?
Answer: A body is said to be in motion when its position changes with time relative to a reference point.
Example: A cargo ship travelling from Chennai to Singapore is in motion relative to the Earth.
Q15. What is distance?
Answer: Distance is the total length of the actual path travelled by an object.
It is a scalar quantity.
SI unit: Metre (m).
Q16. What is displacement?
Answer: Displacement is the shortest straight-line change in position from the initial point to the final point, including direction.
It is a vector quantity.
SI unit: Metre (m).
Q17. Differentiate between distance and displacement.
Answer:
Distance	Displacement
Total path travelled	Shortest straight-line change in position
Scalar quantity	Vector quantity
Has magnitude only	Has magnitude and direction
Always non-negative	Can be positive, negative or zero in one-dimensional motion
Q18. What is speed?
Answer: Speed is the distance travelled per unit time.
Formula:
Speed=DistanceTimeSpeed=\frac{Distance}{Time}
SI unit: Metre per second (m/s).
Q19. What is velocity?
Answer: Velocity is the rate of change of displacement with respect to time.
Velocity=DisplacementTimeVelocity=\frac{Displacement}{Time}
SI unit: Metre per second (m/s).
Q20. What is acceleration?
Answer: Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity with respect to time.
Formula:
a=v−uta=\frac{v-u}{t}
Where:
    • a = Acceleration.
    • v = Final velocity.
    • u = Initial velocity.
    • t = Time.
SI unit: m/s².

SECTION 3: MOTION AND EQUATIONS OF MOTION (Q21–Q30)
Q21. What is uniform motion?
Answer: Uniform motion occurs when an object travels equal distances in equal intervals of time.
Example: A ship travelling at a constant speed of 10 knots under ideal conditions.
Q22. What is non-uniform motion?
Answer: Non-uniform motion occurs when an object travels unequal distances in equal intervals of time or when its velocity changes.
Example: A ship increasing its speed while leaving a harbour.
Q23. What is uniform acceleration?
Answer: Uniform acceleration occurs when the velocity of an object changes by equal amounts in equal intervals of time.
Q24. What is retardation?
Answer: Retardation is negative acceleration, meaning that the velocity decreases in a chosen direction.
Example: A ship slowing down while approaching a port.

SECTION 4: FORCE AND NEWTON'S LAWS OF MOTION (Q31–Q40)
Q31. What is force?
Answer: Force is an external influence that can change or tend to change the state of rest or motion of an object or deform it.
SI unit: Newton (N).
Q32. What are the different types of forces?
Answer:
Some common types of forces are:
    1. Gravitational force.
    2. Frictional force.
    3. Normal force.
    4. Tension force.
    5. Magnetic force.
    6. Electrostatic force.
    7. Buoyant force.
    8. Applied force.
Q33. State Newton's First Law of Motion.
Answer: Newton's First Law states that an object remains at rest or continues in uniform straight-line motion unless acted upon by an unbalanced external force.
It is also known as the Law of Inertia.
Maritime example: A ship does not automatically stop moving when its propulsion is reduced or stopped. Its motion continues because of inertia, although water resistance gradually slows it down.
Q34. What is inertia?
Answer: Inertia is the tendency of a body to resist any change in its state of rest or uniform motion.
Greater mass generally means greater inertia.
Q35. State Newton's Second Law of Motion.
Answer: Newton's Second Law states that the rate of change of momentum of a body is proportional to the applied net force and occurs in the direction of that force.
For constant mass:
F=ma\boxed{F=ma}
Where:
    • F = Force.
    • m = Mass.
    • a = Acceleration.
Q36. A body of mass 10 kg accelerates at 3 m/s². Calculate the force.
Answer:
Given:
m = 10 kg.
a = 3 m/s².
Formula:
F=maF=maF=10×3F=10\times3F=30 N\boxed{F=30\,N}
Q37. State Newton's Third Law of Motion.
Answer: Newton's Third Law states:
"For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction."
Maritime example: A ship's propeller accelerates water backwards, and the resulting reaction produces forward thrust on the ship.
Q38. What is momentum?
Answer: Momentum is the product of the mass and velocity of a moving body.
Formula:
p=mvp=mv
SI unit:
kg m/skg\,m/s
Q39. What is impulse?
Answer: Impulse is the product of force and the time interval for which the force acts.
Formula:
Impulse=F×tImpulse=F\times t
It is equal to the change in momentum.
SI unit: Newton-second (Ns).
Q40. What is friction?
Answer: Friction is a force that opposes relative motion or the tendency of relative motion between surfaces in contact.
Types include:
    1. Static friction.
    2. Sliding friction.
    3. Rolling friction.
    4. Fluid friction.
Maritime example: Water resistance acting against a ship's movement is a form of fluid resistance.

SECTION 5: WORK, ENERGY AND POWER (Q41–Q50)
Q41. What is work?
Answer: Work is done when a force produces displacement.
For a constant force acting in the direction of displacement:
W=F×sW=F\times s
SI unit: Joule (J).
Q42. When is work said to be zero?
Answer: Work is zero when:
    1. There is no displacement.
    2. The applied force is perpendicular to the displacement.
Mathematically:
W=Fscos⁡θW=Fs\cos\theta
Q43. What is energy?
Answer: Energy is the capacity of a body or system to do work.
SI unit: Joule (J).
Q44. What is kinetic energy?
Answer: Kinetic energy is the energy possessed by an object due to its motion.
Formula:
KE=12mv2KE=\frac12mv^2
Q45. A body of mass 10 kg moves at 5 m/s. Calculate its kinetic energy.
Answer:
KE=12mv2KE=\frac12mv^2=12×10×52=\frac12\times10\times5^2=5×25=5\times25KE=125 J\boxed{KE=125\,J}
Q46. What is potential energy?
Answer: Potential energy is the energy possessed by an object due to its position or configuration.
Gravitational potential energy near Earth's surface is:
PE=mghPE=mgh
Where:
    • m = Mass.
    • g = Acceleration due to gravity.
    • h = Height.
Q47. A 5 kg object is raised to a height of 10 metres. Calculate its potential energy. Take g = 9.8 m/s².
Answer:
PE=mghPE=mgh=5×9.8×10=5\times9.8\times10PE=490 J\boxed{PE=490\,J}
Q48. State the law of conservation of energy.
Answer: Energy can neither be created nor destroyed. It can only be transformed from one form into another.
The total energy of an isolated system remains constant.
Q49. What is power?
Answer: Power is the rate of doing work or transferring energy.
Formula:
P=WtP=\frac{W}{t}
SI unit: Watt (W).
Q50. A machine performs 1,000 Joules of work in 10 seconds. Calculate its power.
Answer:
P=100010P=\frac{1000}{10}P=100 W\boxed{P=100\,W}

SECTION 6: GRAVITY AND GRAVITATION (Q51–Q60)
Q51. What is gravity?
Answer: Gravity is the attractive force between objects having mass.
On Earth, gravity attracts objects towards the Earth's centre.
Q52. What is gravitation?
Answer: Gravitation is the universal attractive force acting between any two objects having mass.
Q53. State Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation.
Answer: Every two objects in the universe attract each other with a force directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centres.
Formula:
F=Gm1m2r2F=G\frac{m_1m_2}{r^2}
Where:
    • F = Gravitational force.
    • G = Universal gravitational constant.
    • m₁ and m₂ = Masses.
    • r = Distance between their centres.
Q54. What is the universal gravitational constant?
Answer: The universal gravitational constant is represented by G.
Its approximate value is:
G=6.674×10−11 Nm2/kg2\boxed{G=6.674\times10^{-11}\,Nm^2/kg^2}
Q55. What is acceleration due to gravity?
Answer: Acceleration due to gravity is the acceleration experienced by a freely falling object due to gravitational attraction.
Near Earth's surface:
g=9.81 m/s2\boxed{g=9.81\,m/s^2}
Q56. What is the difference between mass and weight?
Answer:
Mass	Weight

Remains constant for a given body	Can change with gravitational field

Answer: Weight is the gravitational force acting on an object.
Formula:
W=mgW=mg
Where:
    • W = Weight.
    • m = Mass.
    • g = Acceleration due to gravity.

Answer:
W=mgW=mgW=50×9.8W=50\times9.8W=490 N\boxed{W=490\,N}
Q59. Why is gravity important for ships?
Answer: Gravity is important because it affects:
    1. The weight of a ship.
    2. The centre of gravity.
    3. Cargo distribution.
    4. Ship stability.
    5. The ship's tendency to return towards an upright position.
Q60. What is the centre of gravity?
Answer: The centre of gravity is the point through which the resultant gravitational force (weight) of a body acts.
In ship stability, the position of the centre of gravity is extremely important.

SECTION 7: PRESSURE AND ITS APPLICATIONS (Q61–Q70)
Q61. What is pressure?
Answer: Pressure is the normal force acting per unit area.
Formula:
P=FA\boxed{P=\frac{F}{A}}
SI unit: Pascal (Pa).
Q62. Define one Pascal.
Answer: One Pascal is the pressure produced when a normal force of one Newton acts uniformly over an area of one square metre.
1 Pa=1 N/m21\,Pa=1\,N/m^2
Q63. What are the factors affecting pressure?
Answer: For a given normal force:
    1. Applied force.
    2. Area over which the force acts.
Pressure increases when force increases and decreases when area increases.
Q64. A force of 200 N acts on an area of 4 m². Calculate the pressure.
Answer:
P=FAP=\frac{F}{A}P=2004P=\frac{200}{4}P=50 Pa\boxed{P=50\,Pa}
Q65. What is atmospheric pressure?
Answer: Atmospheric pressure is the pressure exerted by the weight of the Earth's atmosphere.
Standard atmospheric pressure at sea level is approximately:
101325 Pa\boxed{101325\,Pa}
It is also approximately 1.01325 bar.
Q66. What is hydrostatic pressure?
Answer: Hydrostatic pressure is the pressure exerted by a fluid at rest due to the weight of the fluid above a given point.
Formula:
P=ρgh\boxed{P=\rho gh}
Where:
    • ρ = Fluid density.
    • g = Acceleration due to gravity.
    • h = Depth below the fluid surface.
Q67. Why does water pressure increase with depth?
Answer: Water pressure increases with depth because the amount and weight of water above a point increase as the point becomes deeper.
Q68. Calculate the gauge pressure at a depth of 10 metres in freshwater. Take density = 1,000 kg/m³ and g = 9.81 m/s².
Answer:
Given:
ρ=1000 kg/m3\rho=1000\,kg/m^3g=9.81 m/s2g=9.81\,m/s^2h=10 mh=10\,m
Formula:
P=ρghP=\rho ghP=1000×9.81×10P=1000\times9.81\times10P=98100 Pa\boxed{P=98100\,Pa}
Therefore, the gauge pressure is 98.1 kPa.
Q69. What is the difference between gauge pressure and absolute pressure?
Answer:
Gauge pressure: Pressure measured relative to atmospheric pressure.
Absolute pressure: Total pressure measured relative to a perfect vacuum.
Relationship:
Pabsolute=Patmospheric+PgaugeP_{absolute}=P_{atmospheric}+P_{gauge}
Q70. What is a manometer?
Answer: A manometer is an instrument used to measure fluid pressure by balancing it against the pressure exerted by a column of liquid.
It is commonly used in engineering and fluid systems.

SECTION 8: FLUID MECHANICS (Q71–Q80)
Q71. What is Fluid Mechanics?
Answer: Fluid Mechanics is the branch of Physics that deals with the behaviour of fluids, including liquids and gases, both at rest and in motion.
It is very important in Nautical Science because ships operate in water.
Q72. What is a fluid?
Answer: A fluid is a substance that continuously deforms under an applied shear stress, however small that stress may be.
Liquids and gases are fluids.
Examples:
    • Water.
    • Oil.
    • Air.
    • Seawater.
Q73. What are the main branches of Fluid Mechanics?
Answer:
    1. Fluid Statics: Study of fluids at rest.
    2. Fluid Kinematics: Study of fluid motion without considering the forces causing it.
    3. Fluid Dynamics: Study of fluids in motion and the forces associated with their motion.
Q74. What is density?
Answer: Density is the mass per unit volume of a substance.
Formula:
ρ=mV\boxed{\rho=\frac{m}{V}}
SI unit:
kg/m3kg/m^3
Q75. What is the approximate density of freshwater and seawater?
Answer:
Typical approximate values are:
    • Freshwater: 1,000 kg/m³.
    • Seawater: Approximately 1,025 kg/m³.
Actual seawater density varies with temperature, salinity and pressure.
Q76. What is viscosity?
Answer: Viscosity is the property of a fluid that represents its resistance to flow or internal deformation.
Example:
Honey has higher viscosity than water.
Q77. What is surface tension?
Answer: Surface tension is the property of a liquid surface that makes it behave as though it were under tension due to cohesive intermolecular forces.
Its SI unit is N/m.
Q78. What is laminar flow?
Answer: Laminar flow is a type of fluid flow in which fluid particles move in relatively smooth, orderly layers with little mixing between adjacent layers.
Q79. What is turbulent flow?
Answer: Turbulent flow is a type of fluid flow characterised by irregular fluctuations, mixing and eddies.
Example: Water flowing rapidly and irregularly around obstacles.
Q80. What is the difference between laminar and turbulent flow?
Answer:

SECTION 9: BUOYANCY, ARCHIMEDES' PRINCIPLE AND FLUID LAWS (Q81–Q90)
This section is particularly important for Nautical Science because it explains the scientific principles behind why ships float.
Q81. What is buoyancy?
Answer: Buoyancy is the upward force exerted by a fluid on a body partially or completely immersed in it.
This force is called the buoyant force or upthrust.
Q82. What is Archimedes' Principle?
Answer: Archimedes' Principle states:
"An object wholly or partially immersed in a fluid experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by the object."
Mathematically:
FB=ρgVF_B=\rho gV
Where:
    • Fᵦ = Buoyant force.
    • ρ = Fluid density.
    • g = Acceleration due to gravity.
    • V = Volume of displaced fluid.
Q83. Why does a steel ship float even though steel is denser than water?
Answer: A steel ship floats because its hollow hull displaces a large volume of water.
The displaced water produces an upward buoyant force.
When the ship floats in equilibrium:
Buoyant Force=Weight of Ship\boxed{Buoyant\ Force=Weight\ of\ Ship}
The average density of the ship, including its enclosed air spaces, is less than the density of the surrounding water.
Q84. What is displacement in relation to ships?
Answer: Ship displacement is the weight of water displaced by a floating ship.
For a floating vessel in equilibrium, displacement equals the vessel's total weight.
It is generally expressed in tonnes.
Q85. What is the difference between freshwater and seawater buoyancy?
Answer: Seawater is generally denser than freshwater.
Therefore, for the same displaced volume, seawater produces a greater buoyant force.
A ship generally floats at a smaller draught in seawater than in freshwater when its weight remains unchanged.
Q86. What is the principle of flotation?
Answer: The principle of flotation states that a floating body displaces a quantity of fluid whose weight is equal to the weight of the floating body.
Q87. State Pascal's Law.
Answer: Pascal's Law states that pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally and undiminished in all directions throughout the fluid and to the walls of its container.
Applications:
    • Hydraulic lifts.
    • Hydraulic brakes.
    • Hydraulic steering systems.
Q88. State Bernoulli's Principle.
Answer: Bernoulli's Principle states that for steady, incompressible and non-viscous flow along a streamline, the total mechanical energy per unit volume remains constant.
The equation is:
P+12ρv2+ρgh=ConstantP+\frac12\rho v^2+\rho gh=Constant
Where:
    • P = Pressure.
    • ρ = Fluid density.
    • v = Fluid velocity.
    • g = Acceleration due to gravity.
    • h = Height.
Q89. What is the continuity equation?
Answer: The continuity equation is based on the conservation of mass in fluid flow.
For steady incompressible flow:
A1v1=A2v2\boxed{A_1v_1=A_2v_2}
Where:
    • A₁ and A₂ = Cross-sectional areas.
    • v₁ and v₂ = Fluid velocities.
Q90. What is the Reynolds number?
Answer: Reynolds number is a dimensionless quantity used to predict the flow characteristics of a fluid.
Formula:
Re=ρvDμRe=\frac{\rho vD}{\mu}
Where:
    • ρ = Fluid density.
    • v = Characteristic velocity.
    • D = Characteristic length or pipe diameter.
    • μ = Dynamic viscosity.
It helps distinguish between laminar, transitional and turbulent flow in suitable flow conditions.

Q91. Why does a ship experience resistance when moving through water?

    2. Wave-making resistance.

Q100. Explain scientifically why a ship does not sink when it carries heavy cargo.
Answer:
A ship does not sink simply because it carries heavy cargo because its hull displaces water and experiences an upward buoyant force.
According to Archimedes' Principle, the buoyant force equals the weight of the displaced water.
When cargo is loaded:
    1. The total weight of the ship increases.
    2. The ship moves deeper into the water.
    3. It displaces more water.
    4. The buoyant force increases.
    5. A new floating equilibrium may be established.
However, the ship must remain within its permitted loading limits and maintain adequate stability and freeboard.
Overloading can cause dangerous loss of freeboard, excessive draught and stability problems.
